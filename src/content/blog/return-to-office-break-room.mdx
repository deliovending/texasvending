**Short answer:** Employees coming back to the office expect a reason to be there, and food on site is one of the simplest. For uneven, hybrid attendance, self-service equipment works best: AI smart coolers or a micro market with fresh meals, plus a coffee station, stocked to match the days people actually come in. At qualifying Dallas–Fort Worth offices, the equipment costs nothing.

More companies are asking people back to the office more days a week. As budgets for 2027 come together, the break room is a line item worth a second look: it is where people decide whether the commute was worth it.

## What Do the Surveys Say?

DoorDash's 2026 Meal Impact Report surveyed 1,019 U.S. office administrators, finance managers, and HR and benefits leaders. It found:

- **54%** say meal program expectations, and their costs, have increased.
- **44%** rank employee satisfaction as the top priority for their meal program, compared with **31%** who put cost first.
- **46%** compare meal benefits with other perks, such as commuter benefits, wellness perks, or phone stipends.

The takeaway is not that every office needs a catered lunch. It is that employees notice whether the office feeds them, and employers are measuring it.

## Why Is Hybrid Attendance Hard to Feed?

- **Uneven days.** Tuesday is packed and Friday is quiet. Catering for the peak wastes food on the quiet days.
- **Changing headcount.** Policies shift, and attendance shifts with them.
- **Lunch spots nearby may not fit.** In many suburban office parks, the nearest lunch is a drive away.

## Does a Hybrid Office Qualify for Free Placement?

With Texas Vending, a location qualifies with **at least 50 people in total foot traffic, counting employees and visitors together**. For a hybrid office, count everyone who uses the space, plus clients and other regular visitors. Our [free vending machine requirements](/blog/free-vending-machine-requirements/) post shows how.
