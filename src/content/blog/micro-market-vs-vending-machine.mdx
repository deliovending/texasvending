## Micro Market vs Vending Machine for an Office: How Do They Compare?

| | Vending machine | AI smart cooler | Micro market |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Selection** | A set number of slots | More than a machine, any package shape | The widest, on open shelves and coolers |
| **Fresh food** | Limited; refrigerated machines can hold some | Yes: sandwiches, salads, meals | Yes, the most variety |
| **Footprint** | One machine | About one refrigerator | A wall or a small room, plus a kiosk |
| **Security and theft exposure** | Products locked behind glass | Door unlocks with a card or phone, and the cooler charges for what is taken | Open shelves; best for closed workplaces with a known group of shoppers |
| **Best fit (setting)** | Small break areas, second floors, spots near a dock or lobby | Small or secured spaces that still want fresh food | Main break rooms where people stay on site for a full shift |
| **Payment** | Cashless: pay, then the item drops | Cashless: tap to unlock, charged when the door closes | Cashless: scan and pay at a kiosk |
| **Hours** | Whenever your building is open | Works around the clock | Open around the clock |

One number applies to all three columns: a location qualifies for free placement with at least 50 people in total foot traffic, counting employees and visitors together. Past that, the choice comes down to space, food, and how your office works.

## When Should an Office Choose a Vending Machine?

A [vending machine](/our-services/vending-machine/) is the right call when:

- **Space is tight.** A snack, drink, or combo machine fits where a store will not.
- **People want a quick snack, not a meal.** Chips, candy, protein bars, and cold drinks cover most afternoon cravings.
- **You need a second food point.** On a multi-floor office, a machine near the far elevator or a lobby saves a walk.
- **The area is open to the public.** Products stay locked behind glass, which suits lobbies and waiting areas.

## When Should an Office Choose a Micro Market?

A [micro market](/our-services/micro-markets/) is the stronger choice when:

- **People eat lunch at their desks or in the break room.** Sandwiches, salads, wraps, and ready-to-eat meals turn a snack stop into a lunch option.
- **You have a real break room.** An open store needs a wall or a corner and room for people to browse.
- **Your team is a known group.** Badge-access floors and closed offices suit open shelves.
- **You want variety.** People can pick up, read, and compare items before they buy.

Many [corporate offices](/industries-we-serve/corporate-offices/) pair a micro market with coffee service. Coffee machine installation is free; coffee and supplies are billed.

## Where Does an AI Smart Cooler Fit?

The smart cooler is the in-between option. It holds fresh meals, drinks, and snacks in about the floor space of a refrigerator. People tap a card or phone to unlock the door, take what they want, and the cooler charges automatically when they close it. It suits a small kitchenette, a secured floor, or an office that wants fresh food but has no room for a full market. ## How Much Space Do I Need for a Micro Market?

There is no single size. A micro market can be a compact wall with one cooler and a snack rack, or a full room with several coolers and shelving runs. What drives the space:

- **How many coolers and shelves.** More fresh food and drinks means more glass-door coolers. Snack selection drives the shelving.
- **The checkout kiosk.** It needs its own spot, close to the products, with room to stand and pay.
- **Clear space in front.** Cooler doors need room to swing open, and people need room to stand while they browse without blocking the walkway.
- **Power and air.** Each cooler needs a nearby outlet without extension cords, and refrigerated equipment needs air around it.
- **Accessibility.** Under the 2010 ADA Standards, accessible operable parts such as a kiosk screen or card reader need a clear floor space of at least 30 by 48 inches in front of them, within a reach range of 15 to 48 inches above the floor. Objects with leading edges between 27 and 80 inches high may protrude no more than 4 inches into a circulation path. Our [ADA vending machine requirements](/blog/ada-vending-machine-requirements/) post covers the details.

## What About Theft in an Office Micro Market?

It is a fair question. A vending machine keeps every item behind glass. A micro market trusts shoppers with open shelves, which is why it is designed for closed workplaces where the people shopping are a known group of employees. Texas rules for self-service food markets require 24/7 video surveillance of shoppers, with records kept for 14 days, and tamper-evident packaging on prepackaged food. If your break room is open to the public, a vending machine or a smart cooler is the safer fit.
