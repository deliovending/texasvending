**Short answer:** A multi-tenant office building can add vending, smart coolers, or a micro market as a shared amenity at no cost if the building has at least 50 people in total foot traffic, counting tenant employees and visitors. Put open markets on badge-access floors or tenant lounges, and smart coolers or vending machines in public lobbies. The vending company owns, stocks, and services the equipment.

Tenants returning to the office expect more from the building than a parking spot and an elevator. Food on site is one of the easiest amenities to add, and one of the few that costs the owner nothing. Here is how property managers in Dallas–Fort Worth set it up.

## Why Add Vending as a Building Amenity?

- **Tenant retention.** A building with food on site is easier to stay in and easier to lease.
- **No buildout cost.** At a qualifying building, the vending company pays for the equipment, delivery, and setup.
- **No staffing.** Cashless, self-service equipment needs no cashier and no one to count money.
- **Fewer complaints.** One vendor for the whole building, with one number to call, beats each tenant bringing its own snack setup.

## Who Counts Toward the 50?

In a multi-tenant building, count everyone who could buy:

- Employees of every tenant who work on site
- Building staff: engineering, security, janitorial
- Regular visitors: clients, patients, couriers, and contractors

Add them into one total. Many buildings reach 50 with two or three mid-size tenants. Our [free vending machine requirements](/blog/free-vending-machine-requirements/) post shows how to count.

## Where Should the Equipment Go?

| Space | Who uses it | Best fit |
|---|---|---|
| Public lobby | Tenants, visitors, the public | Vending machine or [smart cooler](/our-services/ai-smart-cooler/) |
| Tenant lounge or amenity floor | Badge-holding tenants | [Micro market](/our-services/micro-markets/) or smart coolers |
| Inside one tenant's suite | That tenant's staff | Any, sized to the tenant |
| Fitness center | Members | Smart cooler with drinks and protein snacks |

Smart coolers and vending machines only open after a card tap or payment, so they suit public space. An open micro market fits a badge-access area, because Texas describes unstaffed self-service markets as intended for closed locations with a known group of employees. See [Texas micro market rules](/blog/texas-micro-market-rules/).

## What Does the Building Provide?

At a qualifying location, the owner's side is small:

1. **Floor space** in a common area people already pass.
2. **A power outlet** near each machine, and the electricity it uses.
3. **Access** for service visits. We plan visits around your building's check-in and badge rules.

The vending company handles everything else: equipment, stocking, cashless payments, and repairs. Local technicians respond to reported issues within 24 hours, including after hours.

## What Should Tenants Be Able to Buy?

A building serves many companies, so variety matters more than in a single office:

- Fresh sandwiches, salads, and ready-to-eat meals for lunch at the desk
- Snacks and better-for-you options
- Cold drinks and coffee drinks
