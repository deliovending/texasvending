**Short answer:** The best office Christmas party snacks mix festive sweets (peppermint, gingerbread, holiday cookies) with savory snacks, hot cocoa or seasonal coffee, better-for-you picks, and clearly labeled allergy-friendly treats. Plan for every shift, not just the people at the party, and ask your vending company in advance to stock seasonal items in the break room.

The last week before Christmas is short and busy in Dallas–Fort Worth offices. Christmas Day 2026 falls on a Friday, so many teams squeeze the party, the year-end push, and a half-empty office into four days. The snacks don't have to be complicated. This list keeps them simple.

For bigger-picture ideas, like coffee for long weeks, peak-season shifts, and holiday closures, see our [holiday break room ideas](/blog/holiday-break-room-ideas/).

## What Are the Best Office Christmas Party Snacks?

Cover a few types so everyone finds something. Seasonal items depend on what manufacturers release each year, so treat flavors below as ideas, not a guaranteed lineup.

### Seasonal flavors

- Peppermint bark, candy canes, and mint chocolate
- Gingerbread cookies and gingersnaps
- Sugar cookies with holiday icing
- Pumpkin spice and eggnog flavors, when you can find them

### Hot cocoa and seasonal coffee

A warm drink station turns a snack table into a reason to linger. Set out hot cocoa, tea, and coffee with plenty of cups and creamer. Several coffees on our [office coffee menu](/fresh-food/coffee-menu/) have chocolate or cocoa tasting notes, which pair well with holiday sweets.

### Shareable boxes

Cookie tins, popcorn tins, and boxed chocolates work well for a party and for the days around it. Individually wrapped treats are easier for people who stop by between calls and don't want to reach into a shared bowl.

### Savory options

Holiday tables lean sweet. Balance them with cheese and crackers, pretzels, nuts, chips, and a tray of sandwiches or wraps. People who skipped lunch will thank you.

### Better-for-you options

Fruit, yogurt parfaits, nuts, sparkling water, and zero-sugar drinks give people a lighter choice. Our [beverage catalog](/beverages/) lists the sugar and calories in the drinks we stock most often.

### Allergy-friendly options

Under U.S. law, packaged foods must declare nine major food allergens: milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, and sesame. Sesame was added on January 1, 2023, so older habits may miss it. A few easy rules:

- Keep packaged treats in their wrappers so people can read the label.
- Put anything with nuts on its own tray, away from nut-free items.
- Label homemade treats with their ingredients, or skip them.
- Include at least one treat that is free of the most common allergens.

## How Much Should You Plan For?

There is no formula that fits every office, but a few questions get you close:

- **Who is on site that day?** Count employees, contractors, and visitors, not just the people on the invite.
- **Who misses the party?** People on calls, on other shifts, or working remotely that day may still want a treat later. Set some aside.
- **How long is the party?** A long afternoon event needs more savory food than a 30-minute cookie break.
- **What keeps?** Packaged snacks hold up if you over-order. Fresh food doesn't, so order it close to the day and in smaller amounts.

A balanced table usually has something from each group:

1. One or two festive sweets, such as gingerbread and something peppermint
2. One savory tray, such as cheese and crackers or pretzels and nuts
3. One lighter choice, such as fruit or yogurt
4. One clearly labeled allergy-friendly treat
5. A warm drink and a cold one, with water on the side

Five groups, a few items each. That covers most tastes without filling a conference room with leftovers.

## When Should You Order Christmas Snacks for the Office?

Earlier than you think, especially for anything seasonal.

- **Early December:** ask your vending company about seasonal items you want in the break room. Limited holiday products come and go, and new items need time to arrive.
- **Two weeks out:** confirm the party date, headcount, and any office closures so stocking matches the calendar.
- **The last week before Christmas:** order fresh trays for the day of the party and lean on packaged snacks for the rest of the week. Before a long closure, let fresh food sell down.

Reading this the week of your party? Packaged treats, a warm drink station, and a stocked break room still make a good spread.
