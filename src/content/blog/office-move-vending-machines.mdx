Office moves have hundreds of line items. The break room is often the last one anyone thinks about, until move-in day, when the coolers are still at the old address or the machine in the new kitchen will not turn on. Here is how to avoid that.

## Why Are Vending Machines Hard to Move?

A vending machine is not a filing cabinet:

- **Weight and balance.** Machines are heavy and top-heavy. Tipping one is a serious injury risk.
- **Refrigeration.** Drink machines and smart coolers have compressors and refrigerant lines that need careful handling.
- **Electronics.** Card readers, touchscreens, and network connections need to be powered down and reconnected correctly.
- **Ownership.** In many offices, the vending company owns the equipment. Your movers may not be allowed to touch it.

## What Is the Timeline for a Break Room Move?

### Four to six weeks before the move

- **List the equipment:** every vending machine, smart cooler, micro market cooler and kiosk, coffee brewer, and water system.
- **Confirm who owns each item.** Vendor-owned equipment needs the vendor's sign-off.
- **Get the new floor plan.** Mark where the break room goes and how people will reach it.

### Two to four weeks before

- **Schedule the equipment move** with the vending or moving company, and line it up with your general movers.
- **Check the path.** Measure doors, hallways, elevators, and turns between the dock and the new break room.
- **Check power and network.** Confirm outlets at each machine's new spot. Ask building management about after-hours access and elevator reservations.

### Move week

- **Let the vendor sell down fresh food** so nothing perishable rides in a cooler that is switched off.
- **Post a notice** so employees know when the old machines go offline and when the new break room opens.

### Move day and after

- Equipment is disconnected, wrapped, moved, placed, leveled, connected, and tested.
- Refrigerated machines are restocked once they reach a safe temperature.

## What Should You Measure in the New Space?

Most move-day problems come from the new space, not the truck. Before the move, check:

- **Doorways and elevators** along the route, at their narrowest point.
- **Floor space** for each machine, plus room in front for people to stand, pay, and open doors.
- **An outlet near each machine.** Avoid extension cords and power strips.
- **Clearance behind refrigerated machines** so they can vent heat. Each manual lists the number.
- **Traffic flow.** Put the equipment where people already walk, not in a dead-end corner.

## Should You Upgrade While You Move?

A move is the cheapest time to change the setup, because the equipment is already being handled. Common upgrades:

- **Add fresh food.** Swap a snack machine for an [AI smart cooler](/our-services/ai-smart-cooler/) or a full [micro market](/our-services/micro-markets/).
- **Split the setup.** A bigger building may need a second break room, or a cooler near the dock and another near the offices.
- **Retire tired machines.** Old equipment that broke often at the old site will not improve on a truck.
