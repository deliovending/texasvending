**Short answer:** Yes, free vending is real at locations with enough people. The vending company owns the machine and earns its money from product sales, so a qualifying business pays nothing for equipment, delivery, setup, or service. What you do provide is floor space, a power outlet, and the electricity the machine uses. The real risk is not the machine. It is the fees and terms some agreements add later.

"Free" makes buyers suspicious, and that is healthy. This post explains how the model works, what you give in return, and the questions that separate an honest free placement from one that costs you later.

## How Does Free Vending Work?

The vending company buys the equipment, delivers it, installs it, and stocks it. Employees and visitors buy snacks, drinks, and meals at normal prices. Those sales pay for the products, the equipment, and the service visits.

That is why every vendor has a threshold. A machine in a five-person office cannot sell enough to cover a truck visit. With Texas Vending, the threshold is **at least 50 people in total foot traffic, counting employees and visitors together**. Our [free vending machine requirements](/blog/free-vending-machine-requirements/) post shows how to count your site.

## What Does Your Business Provide?

Free placement is a trade, and the host's side is small but real:

1. **Floor space** in a spot where people already walk.
2. **A power outlet** near that spot, and the electricity the machine uses.
3. **Access** for the service team. We plan visits around your shifts, docks, and badge rules.

That is the full list. You do not buy product, count cash, or call for repairs on a machine you own.

## Where Do Hidden Costs Show Up?

"Free" placement from any vendor can still come with terms that cost money later. Read the agreement for:

- **Sales minimums.** Some agreements charge the host if monthly sales fall below a target.
- **Service or restocking fees** that appear after the first few months.
- **Long terms with auto-renewal.** A three- or five-year term that renews unless you cancel in a narrow window.
- **Early termination and removal fees.** The cost to end the agreement or have the equipment taken away.
- **Exclusivity.** A clause that blocks you from adding a coffee service or a second vendor.
