January is when people make resolutions, and the break room can make a few too. A new year is a natural moment to fix the machine that only takes dollar bills, the coffee maker that leaks, and the cooler that has been half empty since October. Here is a simple plan.

## Break Room Refresh Ideas Start With an Audit

Walk the break room like a new hire would. Look for:

- **Empty or stale machines.** Rows of sold-out coils, or the same items nobody buys.
- **Cash-only readers.** Many people no longer carry cash. A machine that can't take a card loses the sale.
- **Broken or tired coffee.** A leaking brewer, an empty creamer bin, or a pot that sits all afternoon.
- **No real food.** Chips and candy are a snack, not a lunch.
- **A room nobody stays in.** Bad lighting, too few seats, or equipment in the way.

Write it down. That list becomes your refresh plan.

## What Are Healthy Vending Options for Offices in 2027?

Healthy vending is not about taking the candy away. It is about putting better choices right next to it, so the easy pick can also be the better one.

- **Better-for-you snacks:** nuts, trail mix, protein snacks, baked chips, and bars with less added sugar.
- **Fresh food:** salads, wraps, sandwiches, fruit, yogurt parfaits, and ready-to-eat meals from a smart cooler or micro market.
- **Water:** still and sparkling, easy to see and easy to grab.
- **Low-sugar drinks:** zero-sugar sodas, sports drinks, and teas. Our [beverages page](/beverages/) lists sugar, calories, and caffeine for common drinks.
- **The favorites:** keep them. People are more likely to try something new when their usual is still there.

If you want a reference point, the CDC's *Food Service Guidelines for Federal Facilities* are voluntary best practices for healthier food at work, and the CDC says private worksites can use them too. They recommend options like fruits and vegetables, whole grains, lean and plant-based proteins, and lower-sodium choices, plus freely available drinking water. They also note that what people pick is shaped by how items are placed, presented, promoted, and priced.

## Fix the Room Itself

Food brings people in. The room decides whether they stay. Keep it simple:

- Enough seats for the busiest break, not the average one.
- Good light and a clean table surface.
- A clear path to the equipment, with room to stand in line without blocking the door.

For offices planning around hybrid attendance, our post on [return-to-office break rooms](/blog/return-to-office-break-room/) goes deeper.

## Ask Employees What They Want

The people who use the break room know what is missing. Ask them:

- A short survey: what would you buy, and what do you never buy?
- A sign-up sheet by the coffee station for product requests.
- A check-in after a month to see what changed.
