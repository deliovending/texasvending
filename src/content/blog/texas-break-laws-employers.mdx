**Short answer:** Texas has no state law that requires meal or rest breaks for adult employees. Federal law still decides whether the breaks you give are paid, and it requires break time for nursing employees. Most break rules at a Texas worksite come from your own policy.

This guide is for facilities managers, HR teams, and operations leads at Dallas–Fort Worth offices, warehouses, and plants. It is general information, not legal advice. For a decision about your own policy, talk to an employment attorney.

## Does Texas Require Lunch Breaks?

No. The U.S. Department of Labor publishes two tables of state break laws: one for [meal periods](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/state/meal-breaks) and one for [paid rest periods](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/state/rest-periods). Texas is not in either table. That means Texas has no state rule for adult employees in the private sector about:

- how long a lunch break must be,
- when in a shift a break must happen, or
- how many rest breaks a shift must include.

Federal law does not require breaks either. But once you give a break, federal rules decide whether you must pay for it.

## What Federal Law Says About the Breaks You Give

The Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) applies to Texas employers. The Department of Labor explains how it treats breaks in [Fact Sheet #22: Hours Worked](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/22-flsa-hours-worked).

### Short Rest Breaks Are Paid Time

Rest periods of about 20 minutes or less "must be counted as hours worked." A 10- or 15-minute break is paid time under federal law.

You do not have to pay for a break that runs longer than you allow. That applies only if you clearly told the employee:

- how long the break may last,
- that going over is against your rules, and
- that going over will be disciplined.

### Meal Periods Are Unpaid Only When Workers Are Fully Off Duty

Meal periods of about 30 minutes or more generally do not need to be paid. The catch is that the employee "must be completely relieved from duty." The fact sheet gives an example: an employee who eats at a desk and answers the phone is working, and that time must be paid.

On a warehouse floor, the same rule applies to an employee who must watch a dock door, a radio, or a line while eating. That employee is not fully off duty.

### On-Call Time on Your Premises Counts as Work

The fact sheet also says an employee who must stay on call at your site is working. If your lunch policy keeps people on site and on call, plan to pay for that time.

## The Federal Break Rule Many Employers Miss: Nursing Employees

The PUMP Act was signed into law on December 29, 2022. It expanded the FLSA rules for nursing employees. Under [Fact Sheet #73](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/73-flsa-break-time-nursing-mothers), covered employers must give:

- reasonable break time to express breast milk, for up to one year after the child's birth, and
- a private place, shielded from view, that is not a bathroom.

Pumping breaks must be paid if the employee is not completely relieved from duty. Employers with fewer than 50 employees may be exempt, but only if compliance would cause an undue hardship.

## Why DFW Sites Still Plan Their Breaks Carefully

No state law sets your break schedule, so your break policy is a business decision. If your schedule uses a short paid rest break and an unpaid meal period, every minute of each break matters.

A 15-minute break is short. If the nearest food is a drive away, the break goes to traffic, not rest. Then people come back late, skip the break, or stop eating on shift.

## How to Make a Short Break Count

The fastest fix is to bring food and drinks to where people already are. A well-run break room lets employees spend the whole break eating and resting.

### Put Food Within Walking Distance

### Cover Every Shift, Not Just Days

Night and weekend crews get the same break as day shift. Micro markets are self-checkout and open around the clock, and vending machines and smart coolers need no cashier. Texas Vending plans service visits around your shifts, docks, and badge rules, so restocking does not get in the way of work.

### Stock Real Meals for Meal Periods

A 30-minute meal period needs more than chips. Texas Vending stocks [fresh food](/fresh-food/), including sandwiches, salads, and ready-to-eat meals, plus protein snacks and better-for-you drinks.

### Make Paying Fast

Every Texas Vending machine and market is cashless. Employees pay with cards, mobile wallets, or tap-to-pay, so nobody spends the break looking for an ATM.

## Break Policy Checklist for Facilities Managers

Use this list when you review your break policy with HR or counsel:

1. **Write the lengths down.** State how long rest breaks and meal periods last.
2. **Pay short breaks.** Treat breaks of about 20 minutes or less as paid time.
3. **Fully relieve workers at meals.** If someone must watch a door, line, or phone, pay for that time.
4. **Plan for nursing employees.** Name a private space that is not a bathroom.
5. **Check the walk.** Time how long it takes to get from the floor to food and back.
6. **Cover all shifts.** Confirm that night and weekend crews have the same food access as days.
