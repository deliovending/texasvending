**Short answer:** A micro market is a small self-checkout store inside a workplace. Open shelves and glass-door coolers hold snacks, drinks, and fresh meals. People take what they want and pay at a kiosk with a card, a mobile wallet, or tap-to-pay. No cashier and no cash are needed, and the market stays open around the clock.

Facilities managers hear the term from vendors, HR, and employees who saw one at a previous job. This guide explains what a micro market is, how it works day to day, and when it is the right fit.

## How Does a Micro Market Work?

Picture a convenience store aisle built into your break room:

1. **Shop.** Employees browse open racks of snacks and coolers of drinks and fresh food. They can pick up an item, read the label, and put it back.
2. **Scan.** At the self-checkout kiosk, they scan each item's barcode.
3. **Pay.** They tap or insert a card, or pay with a mobile wallet. The kiosk shows a receipt.
4. **Restock.** The market reports sales in real time, so the vending company knows what is selling and what is running low. A route driver restocks on a set schedule for your location.

## What Does a Micro Market Sell?

A micro market carries far more than a vending machine. Typical items:

- **Fresh food:** sandwiches, salads, wraps, and ready-to-eat meals.
- **Snacks:** chips, candy, protein bars, nuts, and better-for-you options.
- **Cold drinks:** water, sodas, energy drinks, juices, and coffee drinks.
- **Grab-and-go basics** that a team asks for often.

Because sales are tracked by item, the menu can change with what your people actually buy. Texas Vending adds specific products and brands when there is enough demand.

- Mobile wallets on a phone or watch

## What About Theft?

It is the first question most managers ask. Micro markets are designed for "closed" workplaces, where the people shopping are a known group of employees rather than the general public. Texas rules for self-service food markets require 24/7 video surveillance of shoppers, with records kept for 14 days, and tamper-evident packaging on prepackaged food. We cover those rules in detail in a separate post.

## Which Workplaces Are a Good Fit?

Micro markets work best where people stay on site for a full shift and want real food, not just a snack:

- [Corporate offices](/industries-we-serve/corporate-offices/) and break rooms
- [Warehouses](/industries-we-serve/warehouses/) and distribution centers with several shifts
- [Manufacturing plants](/industries-we-serve/manufacturing-facilities/)
- Hospitals, clinics, and other round-the-clock sites

For a smaller space, an [AI smart cooler](/our-services/ai-smart-cooler/) gives people fresh food in the footprint of one fridge. For snacks and drinks only, a [vending machine](/our-services/vending-machine/) may be enough. We recommend a setup after the site walk, not before.
