## What Should You Do Right Now?

### Do not shake, tip, or rock the machine

This one matters most. Vending machines are heavy and top-heavy. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission has reported at least 37 deaths and 113 injuries between 1978 and 1995 from people rocking or tilting vending machines to get free product or money. The industry warning label says it plainly: never rock or tilt.

### Do not reach inside

Keep hands out of the delivery bin past the flap and away from any open panel. A stuck item is not worth a hurt wrist.

### Leave the power alone unless it is unsafe

Do not unplug the machine just because it is acting up. Refrigeration needs to keep running, and the technician needs to see the fault. The exception: sparking, smoke, a burning smell, or water near the outlet. Then keep people back and tell facilities right away.

## What to Do in the Most Common Situations

### The machine took my money

Card and phone payments leave a record. Write down the machine location, the time, the item, and how you paid, including the last four digits of your card. Contact the vending company on the machine. The company that runs the machine is the one that can look into the charge, so that is who needs to hear about it.

### The card reader is down

If the reader says "offline" or will not wake up, check whether the power just blipped. Readers can take a few minutes to reconnect after an outage. If it is still down after that, report it. Every Texas Vending machine is cashless, so a dead reader means a machine nobody can use.

### An item is stuck

The spiral turned, the bag did not drop. Resist the urge to hit the glass. Note the selection number and report it.

### The cooler is warm

This is the urgent one. If drinks feel warm or the fresh food section of a machine or [smart cooler](/our-services/ai-smart-cooler/) is not cold, do not buy or eat fresh food from it. Report it right away and put a sign on it so others skip it too.

### The screen is dark

Check that the machine is plugged in and the breaker has not tripped. If it is still dark, report it.

## How Do You Report a Vending Machine That Is Not Working?

Find the vending company's name and phone number on the machine. It is usually on a sticker on the front or side. Then send:

- **Machine number.** Many machines carry an ID or asset number on a label. It tells the company exactly which unit is down.
- **Location:** building, floor, and room or hallway.
- **A photo** of the front of the machine and any error message on the screen.
- **What happened:** what you tried to buy, what the machine did, and when.
- **Payment details** if you were charged: time, amount, and the last four digits of the card.
- **Access notes:** badge check-in, dock hours, or escort rules for the technician.

## Vending Machine Not Working in Your Dallas Office? Who to Call

**If you are an employee:** call the company on the machine, and tell your office or facilities manager so they know.

**If you are the office manager:** call the vendor. If you do not know who it is, check the sticker, your facilities files, or the last invoice. Keep a list of every machine in the building with its vendor and phone number.

**If the vendor will not answer, or is gone:** call a local repair company. ## When Should You Escalate?

One breakdown is normal. These are signs something bigger is wrong:

- **No response.** You reported it and nobody called back.
- **The same machine keeps failing.** Repeat repairs on the same problem point to old equipment or poor maintenance.
- **Refund requests go nowhere.** Employees stop trusting the machine, and then they stop using it.
- **A safety issue is ignored.** Leaks, burning smells, or a machine that rocks when touched need attention now.

At that point, it is a vendor question, not a repair question. At a location with at least 50 people in total foot traffic, counting employees and visitors, Texas Vending can place and service [vending machines](/our-services/vending-machine/) at no cost, with no long-term contract.
