**Short answer:** There is no single right number for how often vending machines should be restocked. It depends on how many people use the machine, when they work, and what they buy. Texas Vending uses real-time monitoring to see what is selling and what is running low, sets a vending machine restocking schedule for each location, and plans visits around your shifts, docks, and badge rules.

Every vending company promises machines that are "always stocked." Few explain what that means or how they pull it off. Here is how restocking actually works, and what to ask before you trust the promise.

## How Often Should Vending Machines Be Restocked?

Honest answer: it depends. Anyone who gives you a fixed number without seeing your site is guessing. These are the factors that set the pace:

- **Foot traffic.** A machine serving several shifts empties faster than one in a small office.
- **Shifts.** Two or three shifts means sales around the clock and no quiet overnight to catch up.
- **What sells.** A few best sellers often run out long before the rest of the machine does.
- **Fresh food.** Sandwiches, salads, and meals need more attention than chips and soda.
- **Seasons and events.** Peak season, overtime, heat waves, and training weeks all change demand.

A fixed calendar gets this wrong in both directions. Visit too rarely and best sellers run out. Visit too often on a quiet machine and you are paying for a trip to top off three slots. That is why our schedule comes from data, not the calendar.

## What Does "Always Stocked" Really Mean?

It does not mean every coil is full every minute. It means:

- **Best sellers do not run out.** The items people actually walk to the break room for are there.
- **The menu fits the people.** Products that sit are swapped for products that sell.
- **Fresh food is fresh.** Nothing is past its date, and meals do not vanish by noon.
- **Problems get caught early.** A low row or a jammed spiral is flagged before it becomes a complaint.

## Inside Our Vending Machine Restocking Process

Here is how Texas Vending keeps machines, micro markets, and smart coolers stocked.

### 1. The equipment reports sales in real time

Our machines and markets report what sells as it happens. That shows us which items are running low, which ones sell out first, and when a machine needs service, before your team notices an empty row.

### 2. Each location gets its own schedule

We set a restocking schedule for every location based on its sales. Your schedule is not copied from the site down the road.

### 3. Visits are planned around your operation

A restocking visit should not block a dock door during a trailer rush or show up at a badge desk that is not expecting anyone. We plan service visits around your shifts, docks, and badge rules.

### 4. We stock, clean, and check

On a visit, we restock and clean the equipment. If the monitoring flagged a problem, it gets handled. For repairs, our local technicians respond to reported issues within 24 hours, including after hours.

### 5. The menu keeps adjusting

Sales data shows what your people buy, so the mix changes with it. Want something specific? Ask. We add specific products and brands when there is enough demand at your location.

## How Does Restocking Work for Micro Markets and Smart Coolers?

The same way, with more fresh food. A [micro market](/our-services/micro-markets/) is a self-checkout store with open shelves and coolers, open around the clock. An [AI smart cooler](/our-services/ai-smart-cooler/) charges automatically when an item is taken. Both report sales in real time, and both get a restocking schedule set for the location.

Fresh food raises the bar. Any good vendor should do these things on every visit:

- **Rotate by date.** Move older items to the front and newer ones to the back.
- **Pull anything past its date.** Do not leave it for someone to find at 2 a.m.
- **Match quantities to sales.** Order enough meals that they do not sell out by lunch, but not so many that they go to waste.

## How Are Restocking Visits Planned Around Warehouse Shifts?

[Warehouses](/industries-we-serve/warehouses/) and distribution centers are where a careless restock causes real trouble. Dock doors are busy, security is tight, and the break room is busiest at shift change.

A good restocking plan covers:

- **Timing.** Visits avoid shift change and peak dock hours.
- **Access.** The driver knows the badge process, check-in point, and escort rules before showing up.
- **Night shift.** Second and third shift buy too, and their best sellers may differ from day shift. Read more on [feeding the night shift](/blog/night-shift-break-room-food/).

## How Do You Find a DFW Vending Company That Keeps Machines Stocked?

Ask these questions before you sign:

1. **How do you know when my machine is low?** Look for real-time monitoring, not "we check weekly."
2. **How is my restocking schedule set?** It should come from your site's sales.
3. **How do you plan visits around our shifts and security?**
4. **How do you handle fresh food dates?**
5. **Can we request products?** And what happens when we do?
6. **What happens when something breaks?** Get the response time in writing.
