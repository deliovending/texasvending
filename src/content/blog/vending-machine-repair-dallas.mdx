A dead vending machine looks small on a work order. To the people who walk to the break room with ten minutes to eat, it is the whole break. This guide covers what to check, what to report, and how to get the machine back in service quickly.

## What Breaks Most Often?

Most service calls fall into five groups:

- **Card readers** that decline every card, show "offline," or never wake up.
- **Refrigeration** in drink machines, fresh food machines, and smart coolers. Warm drinks are the first sign.
- **Coin mechanisms** that jam or reject coins.
- **Vend failures**, where the machine takes payment but the spiral or elevator does not drop the item.
- **Coffee machines** and other break room equipment that stop brewing or dispensing.

## What Should You Check Before You Call?

A few checks take two minutes and often save a visit:

1. **Is the machine on?** Look for a lit display and running lights. Check the outlet and the breaker if the machine is dark.
2. **Did the power blip?** After an outage, card readers can take several minutes to reconnect. Give it ten minutes before you report it.
3. **Is it only one item?** If one selection fails and others work, the problem is that spiral or slot, not the whole machine.
4. **Is the drink section warm?** Feel a drink. A warm machine needs refrigeration service soon, because product in it may need to be pulled.

Do not open the machine, tilt it, or rock it to free a stuck item. Vending machines are heavy and top-heavy, and tipping one is a real injury risk.

## What Information Speeds Up a Repair?

A clear report lets the technician bring the right parts the first time. Send:

- **Where the machine is:** building, floor, and the room or hallway.
- **What it is:** snack, drink, combo, coffee machine, or smart cooler. A photo of the front helps.
- **What it does:** the exact error message, or what happens when someone tries to buy.
- **When it started**, and whether it followed a power outage, a move, or cleaning.
- **Access rules:** badge check-in, dock hours, escort needs, or times to avoid.

## How Do You Prevent the Next Breakdown?

Preventive maintenance reduces breakdowns and helps equipment last longer. For machines we service, real-time monitoring tells us when a machine needs service, so many problems get fixed before your team notices.

Simple habits help too:

- Keep open space behind refrigerated machines so they can vent heat. The machine's manual lists the exact clearance.
- Do not plug a vending machine into an extension cord or a power strip.
- Keep the area dry. Mops and floor cleaners near the base can damage wiring over time.
- Report small problems early, like one dead spiral or a slow reader, before they turn into a dark machine.
