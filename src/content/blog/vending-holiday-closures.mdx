**Short answer:** To prep vending machines for a holiday closure, tell your vending company your closure and reopening dates and who will still be on site, sort out building access for service visits, ask for the fresh food order to be adjusted before you close, keep refrigerated equipment plugged in, and schedule a restock before reopening day. A ten-minute email in early December saves a lot of empty shelves in January.

The last two weeks of the year are uneven. Some Dallas–Fort Worth offices close for a week or more. Others run with a skeleton crew. Warehouses may be busier than ever. Your vending machines, smart coolers, and micro market don't know any of that unless someone tells the vendor. This checklist is for the office or facilities manager who will be that someone.

## What Should You Tell Your Vending Company Before a Holiday Closure?

Send one message with everything in it:

- **Closure dates.** The first and last day the building is closed or on reduced hours.
- **Reopening day.** The day most people come back. This is the date that matters most.
- **Who will be on site.** Security, maintenance, a skeleton crew, or nobody at all.
- **A contact.** Name and phone number of someone who can answer questions or let a technician in.
- **Any changes to the space.** Holiday parties, furniture moves, or decorations near the equipment.

The earlier you send it, the easier it is to plan. Texas Vending plans service visits around your shifts, docks, and badge rules, and a closure is just another schedule to plan around.

## How Do Service Visits Work When the Building Is Closed?

Closed buildings are harder to get into. Before the break, settle:

- **Badges and keys.** Does the route driver's badge still work during the closure? Some buildings switch access groups off over the holidays.
- **Alarms.** If the alarm is armed, who disarms it, and who gets the call if it trips?
- **Loading dock and freight elevator.** Are they open, and on what hours?
- **Escort rules.** Data centers and secure sites may require an escort for every visit. Make sure someone is available on the days you need a visit.
- **Parking and entrances.** If the usual door is locked, say which one to use.

## What Happens to Fresh Food During a Holiday Closure?

Sandwiches, salads, wraps, and ready-to-eat meals have dates. Chips and canned drinks are fine for a week in an empty office; a salad is not.

- **Ask your vendor to adjust the fresh food order** before the closure, so less dated food is sitting in a cooler nobody will open.
- **Ask how it handles items that reach their date** while you are closed. Policies differ by vendor, so get the answer before the break.
- **Keep some food for whoever is working.** If security or a crew is on site, make sure the cooler still has meals for them.

## Should You Unplug Vending Machines Over the Holidays?

It is tempting to save power while the office is empty. Don't unplug refrigerated equipment without asking your vendor first.

- **Product.** Drink machines, smart coolers, and micro market coolers keep items cold. A machine that sits without power warms up, and what is inside may have to be thrown out.
- **Monitoring.** Real-time monitoring shows when a machine or market needs service. A machine with no power can't report anything.
- **Restarts.** If something was switched off, ask your vendor before turning it back on, so it can be checked.

Tell your cleaning crew and the people putting up decorations too. The vending machine outlet is not the spot for holiday lights.

## Who Is Still Working? Plan for Partial Staffing

A closed office is rarely empty. Think about:

- **Security officers** on overnight and weekend posts.
- **Skeleton crews** covering phones, IT, or maintenance.
- **Warehouses and distribution centers** still running peak season, often with extra shifts.
- **Data centers** that run 24/7 no matter the date.

For these teams, self-service equipment matters most. If your [warehouse](/industries-we-serve/warehouses/) or [data center](/industries-we-serve/data-center/) runs through the holidays, tell your vendor which shifts are working so stocking follows them.

## Vending Machine Holiday Restock Schedule: Plan for Reopening Day

The day everyone comes back is the day the break room gets judged. Plan backward from it:

- **Restock before reopening day,** not on it. Ask your vendor to schedule a visit for the last working day before people return.
- **Fresh food comes back first.** Make sure meals, salads, and breakfast are on the shelves for the first morning.
- **Short weeks still count.** A half-staffed week between holidays may need a lighter visit, not a skipped one.

## How Do You Report a Problem Over the Holidays?

If a machine stops taking cards, a cooler feels warm, or a coil jams, report it right away. Texas Vending local technicians respond to reported service issues within 24 hours, including after hours. Include:

- The building and the machine's location inside it
- What is wrong, and a photo if you can
- Who can let a technician in, if the building is closed

Our post on [vending machine repair in Dallas](/blog/vending-machine-repair-dallas/) covers what to include in more detail.

## Your Vending Machine Holiday Closure Checklist

- Send closure dates, reopening day, on-site staff, and a contact
- Confirm badges, alarms, dock hours, and escort rules
- Ask for the fresh food order to be adjusted before closing
- Leave refrigerated equipment plugged in
- Plan stocking for the people still working
- Schedule a restock before reopening day
- Know how to report a problem
