**Short answer:** The 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design require that where vending machines are provided, at least one of each type is accessible. That means a clear floor space of at least 30 by 48 inches in front of it, operable parts such as the card reader and buttons between 15 and 48 inches above the floor, and controls that work with one hand without tight grasping, pinching, or twisting. Placement matters as much as the machine.

Accessibility questions usually come up during a new build-out, a remodel, or an accessibility review. Vending machines, smart coolers, and micro market kiosks are easy to overlook. This post summarizes the rules that apply. It is general information, not legal advice.

## Which ADA Rule Covers Vending Machines?

Section **228.1** of the 2010 ADA Standards covers depositories, vending machines, change machines, mailboxes, and fuel dispensers. It says that where these are provided, **at least one of each type** must comply with section **309, Operable Parts**.

"Each type" matters. A break room with a snack machine, a drink machine, and a smart cooler has three types. Each needs at least one accessible unit.

## What Does Section 309 Require?

### Clear floor space (309.2 and 305.3)

A clear floor or ground space at least **30 inches by 48 inches** must be in front of the operable parts. It can be positioned for a forward or a side approach, and it must connect to an accessible route.

### Reach range (309.3 and 308)

Operable parts must be within reach:

- **Forward reach, unobstructed:** no higher than **48 inches** and no lower than **15 inches** above the floor.
- **Side reach, unobstructed:** no higher than **48 inches** and no lower than **15 inches** above the floor.

Operable parts include the card reader, keypad, selection buttons, touchscreen, and the product delivery bin.

### Operation (309.4)

Operable parts must work **with one hand** and must not require **tight grasping, pinching, or twisting of the wrist**. The force needed to activate them can be **no more than 5 pounds**.

## Where Do Placement Problems Show Up?

Most accessibility problems are not the machine. They are the spot:

- **No clear space.** A trash can, a table, or a second machine blocks the 30 by 48 inch space.
- **No accessible route.** The machine sits up a step, behind furniture, or down a hall that is too narrow.
- **Protruding into a hallway.** Objects with leading edges between 27 and 80 inches above the floor may protrude **no more than 4 inches** into a circulation path. Machines in corridors should sit in an alcove or leave the route clear.
- **High shelves in a market.** Open shelving above reach range makes some items hard to get. Keep a selection of each product type within reach.

## Who Is Responsible?

The vending company supplies the equipment. The host business controls the space, the route, and where the machine goes. In practice, both have a part:

1. **Before install**, agree on a location with clear floor space and an accessible route.
2. **Ask the vendor** which models meet the reach and operation requirements.
3. **Keep the space clear** after install. Furniture and trash cans tend to drift.
4. **For formal reviews**, work with an accessibility professional or your local building official.
