## Signs It’s Time to Change Your Vending Provider

One bad week happens to everyone. A pattern is different. Watch for these:

- **Empty slots that stay empty.** The same items are sold out visit after visit.
- **Slow repairs.** A broken machine sits for days, and you have to chase the vendor for an update.
- **Refunds that go nowhere.** Employees lose money in a machine and never hear back.
- **Requests that disappear.** Your team asks for a product and nothing changes.
- **Price changes with no explanation.** Prices go up and nobody tells you why.
- **Tired equipment.** Dim lights, smudged glass, cash-only machines, or card readers that work on the third try.
- **No one to call.** You cannot name your contact, or the number goes to a general inbox.

If three or more of these sound like your break room, it is time to look around.

## How to Switch Vending Companies Without a Contract Headache

Most of the hassle in a switch comes from the old agreement, not the new install. Pull out whatever you signed and find four things:

1. **Term and end date.** When does the agreement end, and is there a fee to leave early?
2. **Auto-renewal.** Does it renew automatically unless you cancel inside a certain window?
3. **Notice.** How much notice is required, in what form, and sent to whom?
4. **Removal.** Who removes the equipment, how much notice they need, and whether there is a charge.

## How Does a Vending Company Swap Work?

A clean swap follows the same order every time:

1. **The new provider walks your site.** Texas Vending walks every site before quoting. We look at where people take breaks, the space and power available, and your access rules, and we confirm whether the location qualifies for free placement.
2. **You give notice.** Send it in writing, following your agreement’s terms.
3. **The old vendor removes its equipment.** Those machines belong to the old vendor, so removal is its job. Agree on a date in writing.
4. **The new equipment goes in.** Ask both companies to coordinate so the gap between removal and install is short. Texas Vending installs and stocks the new equipment, and every machine is cashless: cards, mobile wallets, and tap-to-pay.
5. **Tell your team.** Let people know what is changing, how to pay, and how to request products. Texas Vending adds specific products and brands when there is enough demand.

## How Should You Compare Vending Providers?

A switch is a good moment to compare more than the logo on the machine. Put each provider’s answers side by side:

- **Cost and qualification.** What is free, and what does a site need to qualify? At Texas Vending, equipment, delivery, and setup are free at locations with at least 50 people in total foot traffic, counting employees and visitors.
- **Terms.** Contract length, auto-renewal, and removal. Texas Vending does not require a long-term contract.
- **Service response.** A response time in writing, including after hours.
- **Restocking.** Real-time monitoring or a fixed route?
- **Product requests.** How they work and how you hear back.
- **Equipment range.** Can the provider grow with you, from [vending machines](/our-services/vending-machine/) to [micro markets](/our-services/micro-markets/), [AI smart coolers](/our-services/ai-smart-cooler/), and [coffee and water service](/our-services/coffee-water/)?

### What About Vending Prices?

Vending prices are set per location and shown on the machine, so a price list from another site will not tell you much. Ask each provider how prices are set for your location, how often they are reviewed, and how you will hear about changes.
