**Short answer:** Vending machines in Texas K-12 schools that run the federal school meal programs must follow USDA's Smart Snacks in School standards during the school day. In short: snacks up to 200 calories and 200 mg of sodium, entrées up to 350 calories and 480 mg of sodium, no more than 35% of calories from fat, under 10% from saturated fat, and no more than 35% sugar by weight. Drinks are limited by type and size for each grade level.

School administrators, child nutrition directors, and booster clubs all touch vending, and the rules can feel scattered. This guide puts the federal standards and the key Texas policy in one place. It is general information. Your district's wellness policy can be stricter.

## Who Do Smart Snacks Rules Apply To?

Smart Snacks cover **competitive foods**: food and drinks sold to students on campus during the school day outside the reimbursable school meal. That includes vending machines, school stores, snack bars, and a la carte lines.

The **school day** runs from **midnight before to 30 minutes after the end of the official school day**. A machine selling after that window, for example at an evening game, is outside the rule.

Smart Snacks do **not** cover colleges and universities, and generally do not cover machines in staff-only areas that students cannot use.

## What Must a Smart Snack Be?

To be sold, a food must meet **one** of these general standards:

- A grain product with **50% or more whole grains** by weight, or whole grain as the first ingredient
- A first ingredient that is a **fruit, vegetable, dairy, or protein food**
- A **combination food** with at least 1/4 cup of fruit and/or vegetable

## What Are the Nutrient Limits?

It must also meet **all** of these limits:

| Standard | Snack or side | Entrée |
|---|---|---|
| Calories | 200 or less | 350 or less |
| Sodium | 200 mg or less | 480 mg or less |
| Total fat | No more than 35% of calories | No more than 35% of calories |
| Saturated fat | Under 10% of calories | Under 10% of calories |
| Total sugars | No more than 35% by weight | No more than 35% by weight |

Limits apply per item as packaged or served, including any added sauce or topping. The rule lists some exemptions to the fat and sugar limits, such as certain nuts, seeds, and dried fruit.

## What Drinks Can a School Vending Machine Sell?

| Drink | Elementary | Middle | High school |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plain water, plain carbonated water | Any size | Any size | Any size |
| Milk or milk substitutes | Up to 8 oz | Up to 12 oz | Up to 12 oz |
| 100% juice, or juice diluted with water, no added sweeteners | Up to 8 oz | Up to 12 oz | Up to 12 oz |
| Calorie-free flavored water | No | No | Up to 20 oz |
| Other drinks under 5 calories per 8 oz | No | No | Up to 20 oz |
| Other drinks with 40 calories or less per 8 oz | No | No | Up to 12 oz |

Food and drinks for elementary and middle school students must be **caffeine-free**, except for trace amounts that occur naturally. High schools may sell caffeinated items.

## What Are Texas Fundraiser Exempt Days?

USDA lets each state set a policy for school fundraisers that sell food that does not meet the standards. Texas allows **six exempt days per campus per school year**. Even on those days, exempt food cannot be sold during meal service in areas where school meals are sold or eaten. Districts usually require exempt dates to be scheduled in advance through the child nutrition department.

## How Do You Keep a School Machine Compliant?

1. **Agree on a product list** with the child nutrition department before the machine is stocked.
2. **Check the label, not the brand.** Many brands make a Smart Snacks version of a popular snack in a smaller portion.
3. **Separate student and staff areas.** Staff lounges can offer different items if students cannot buy there.
4. **Review the list each semester**, as products and portion sizes change.
