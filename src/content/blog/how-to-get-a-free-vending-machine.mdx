**Short answer:** Here is how to get a free vending machine in Dallas–Fort Worth: confirm your site has at least 50 people in total foot traffic, contact a vending company, and let them walk the site. They recommend the equipment and the menu, then deliver, set up, stock, and service it. You provide the space, a power outlet, and access. With Texas Vending there is no long-term contract.

Most people searching for how to get a free vending machine already know free placement exists. What they want is the process. Who do you call? What happens next? What do you have to do? This post walks through each step, from the first call to the first sale, so nothing surprises you along the way.

## How to Get a Free Vending Machine: The Seven Steps

The whole process fits in seven steps:

1. Check that your site qualifies.
2. Contact the vending company.
3. Walk the site together.
4. Get a recommendation for equipment and a menu.
5. Get your part ready: space, power, and access.
6. Installation and setup.
7. Ongoing restocking and service.

Your part is small. Most of the work sits with the vending company, which is the point of free placement.

## Step 1: Check That Your Site Qualifies

A location qualifies for free placement with at least 50 people in total foot traffic, counting employees and visitors together. Not sure how to count? Our [free vending machine requirements](/blog/free-vending-machine-requirements/) guide explains who counts toward the 50.

## Step 2: Contact the Vending Company

[Reach out](/contact-us/) by phone or through the contact form. A few details make the first conversation faster:

- **Your location:** the address and the type of site (office, warehouse, plant, clinic).
- **Your foot traffic:** employees plus regular visitors. A range is fine.
- **Your hours:** when people are on site, including nights and weekends.
- **What you have now:** nothing, an old machine, or a vendor you want to replace.

You don't need a floor plan or a final spot yet. That comes next.

## Step 3: Walk the Site Together

Texas Vending walks every site before quoting. Someone who knows the building shows us where people take breaks, where the outlets are, and how the team moves between shifts. We also confirm your foot traffic count. Our post on [what happens on a vending site visit](/blog/vending-site-visit/) covers the walk in detail, including what to have ready.

## Step 4: Get a Recommendation for Equipment and a Menu

After the walk, you get a recommendation that fits your space, your hours, and your people. The main options:

- **[Vending machines](/our-services/vending-machine/)** for snacks and drinks in a small footprint, including refrigerated machines for fresh food.
- **[AI smart coolers](/our-services/ai-smart-cooler/)** for fresh food in about the footprint of a refrigerator. The door unlocks with a tap, and the cooler charges automatically for what is taken.
- **[Micro markets](/our-services/micro-markets/)** for a self-checkout store with open shelves and coolers, open around the clock.

Many sites get a mix, such as a micro market in the main break room and a drink machine near the dock.

The menu is part of the recommendation too. Options range from snacks and cold drinks to [fresh food](/fresh-food/) such as sandwiches, salads, wraps, and ready-to-eat meals. Ask for the products your team already likes. Texas Vending adds specific products and brands when there is enough demand.

## Step 5: Get Your Part Ready

Free placement is a trade, and your side of it is short:

1. **Floor space** for the machine, smart cooler, or market.
2. **A power outlet** near that space.
3. **Access** for our team to stock and service the equipment.

That's the full list. You don't buy the equipment, stock it, or handle repairs. Wondering what "free" leaves out? Read [is free vending really free](/blog/is-free-vending-really-free/) for an honest look at what a business pays and the fees to watch for in any agreement.

## Step 6: Installation and Setup

At a qualifying location, the vending company handles the equipment from the truck to the first sale. Texas Vending delivers, places, and sets up the equipment, then stocks it. There is no installation fee.

Every machine, smart cooler, and micro market is cashless. Credit and debit cards, mobile wallets, and tap-to-pay all work, so nobody needs cash on a short break. Prices are set for your location and shown on the machine.

## Step 7: Ongoing Restocking and Service

Placement is the start. Service is what keeps the break room useful:

- **Restocking:** real-time monitoring shows when a machine or market needs service, and the restocking schedule is set for your location.
- **Your schedule:** service visits are planned around your shifts, docks, and badge rules.
- **Repairs:** local technicians respond to reported service issues within 24 hours, including after hours.
- **The menu:** sales are tracked by item, so the menu can follow what your people actually buy.

If your needs change, the setup can change too. Texas Vending also moves machines when you rearrange a floor or change buildings.
